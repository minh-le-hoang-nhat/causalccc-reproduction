Case study 1: CausalCCC network between two immune cell populations in lymph nodes

The first case study infers intracellular pathways between CD4 + (sender) and CD8 + (receiver) T-cell populations in the inguinal lymph nodes under steady-state conditions and after viral infection. The resulting CausalCCC network highlights expected intracellular pathways across these two interacting T-cell populations, using L-R pairs inferred by NicheNet. In the CD4 + sender network, top predicted ligands are associated with antigen presentation to CD8 + T cells via MHC class I H2 proteins, with first-degree neighbors, including Psmb8 , Ifi27l2b , Slfn1 , Shisa5 , Irf7 , Mir703 , and B2m . The potential upstream trigger Stat1 , known to mediate the MHC-I pathway and Type I and II interferon (IFN) signaling in T cells, is found to be a genuine cause of B2m sharing direct or indirect associations with IFN-inducible genes ( e.g. Ifi27l2b , Ifi27l2a , and Ifi47 ) and IFN-stimulated genes ( Isg15 and Isg20 ). During viral infection, these pathways are indeed expected to be upregulated as part of a broad antiviral immune response with active antigen presentation to CD8 + T cells. In the CD8 + receiver network, the Cd8a and Klrd1 receptors of the MHC class I ligands are critical for the activation of CD8 + T-cell cytotoxicity. Downstream interactions with Cd8a and Klrd1 include another subunit of the CD8 complex ( Cd8b1 ) and genes known to transmit signals for T-cell activation and cytokine production, and found in high levels in virus-specific memory CD8 + T cells ( e.g. L y6c2 , L y6c1 , and Ly6ca).
